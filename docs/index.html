Where to look things up for yourself.

Everything on this site is assembled from public records. These are the places those records live, plus the plainest explanations of the legal process I could find.

LOOK UP A CASE

CourtListener FREE

Search millions of court opinions and federal dockets. The RECAP Archive inside it holds filings that people have already paid to unlock — this is the same source the Lawsuits tab reads from. Start here for almost anything.

PACER 10¢ PER PAGE

The federal courts' own records system, and the origin of everything in RECAP. Complete where RECAP has gaps, but you pay per page. Worth an account only if a document you need isn't already free somewhere else.

govinfo FREE

The government's official publishing site. Its U.S. Courts Opinions collection carries authenticated versions of federal opinions, along with the U.S. Code and the Federal Register.

UNDERSTAND THE WORDS

Glossary of Legal Terms — U.S. Courts FREE

The federal judiciary's own dictionary, written for the public. The definitions built into this site come from here. If a word in a docket entry stops you, look it up here first.

Wex — Cornell Legal Information Institute FREE

A free legal encyclopedia maintained by Cornell Law School. Longer than a glossary entry and written by lawyers, but aimed at people who aren't ones. Good when you want the concept rather than the definition.

Oyez FREE

Every Supreme Court case since 1955, with plain-English summaries and the actual audio of the argument. The best place to hear how a case is fought rather than just read how it ended.

SENTENCING AND CRIME DATA

U.S. Sentencing Commission FREE

The real answer to "what sentence do people usually get for this?" Their JSIN tool shows what judges actually imposed for a given offense, and their datafiles record every federal sentence. Far more reliable than generalising from news stories.

Bureau of Justice Statistics FREE

National numbers on arrests, prosecutions, prisons and victims, covering state systems as well as federal. Use it when you want to know whether a case is typical or unusual.

STATE COURTS — THE BIG GAP

National Center for State Courts FREE

Most crimes are charged in state court, and this site holds none of those. There is no national database of state records — each state runs its own. NCSC is the best starting point for finding your state's system.

WHERE THIS SITE'S DATA COMES FROM

Justice Department press releases FREE

Every case under the Sentences tab starts as one of these, written by the prosecutors who brought it. Worth reading in full — they include detail the summaries here leave out, and they are the authority if a number looks wrong.

IF YOU NEED AN ACTUAL LAWYER

LawHelp.org FREE

Nothing on this site is legal advice, and reading about similar cases is no substitute for someone who knows yours. LawHelp connects people to free and low-cost legal aid by state and by problem.

NOTICE

Sentences come from Justice Department press releases and are parsed automatically — read the original before relying on a number.

Filings come from the RECAP Archive, which holds federal court records that someone has already paid to unlock. A filing missing here is not a filing that doesn't exist. Sealed and redacted material never appears, and most trial evidence is never filed publicly at all. General information, not legal advice.
